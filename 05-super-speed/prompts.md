# 05 · Super Speed — prompts

**Context:** You joined Rook two weeks ago as PM on Dispatch.
Release 4.2 shipped on 12 August, just before you arrived, and
landed badly. You've spent three sessions finding out what went
wrong: the two piles of feedback that didn't agree, the numbers that
hid four people inside an average, and the code that settled it —
the shorter timeout is what did it.

At the end of the session, ask Claude Code to save the prompts you
wrote yourself below — not the starter prompt. The closing slide has
the exact prompt to paste. By Module 6 this file is a prompt library
built from your own questions.

---

### 1.

Read director-request.txt and everything in 00-rook/company/ (glossary,
Priya's handoff, the Q3 roadmap, the #dispatch-team Slack thread). Draft a
one-pager brief for Helen that answers exactly what she asked for: not a
setting, something Kip would notice on the console, and something a
responder who's gone quiet — use Meteor Mite from Module 3 — would feel
differently about. Cover who it's for, what changes for them, and what it
deliberately doesn't do. Use Rook's real terms and names throughout, not
generic placeholders.

### 2.

Now push on your own brief: write three questions an engineer would need
answered before they'd call this actually buildable — not generic PM
questions, ones specific to the routing mechanism we found in
dispatch-routing (the 0.12/0.08 scoring asymmetry, the missing decay, the
closed loop with no exit).

### 3.

Helen said "even better" if she can click through it. Turn the brief into
something she can actually click — a before/after walkthrough of Kip's
console and Meteor Mite's phone across the days this would play out, not
just a static mockup — and publish it somewhere I can send her a link.

### 4.

The console panel in the prototype shows Meteor Mite next to a generic
climbing responder (Stormwrack). Kip's actual interview names the real
pair: Meteor Mite and The Gale, the two cards Kip has open side by side
every shift, describing them as looking like "two different products."
Rebuild that panel around the real pair, using the actual weekly
pings-sent/accepted numbers from callout-history.csv for both — 11/7
the week 4.2 shipped collapsing to 1/0 by 31 Aug for Meteor Mite, 13/10
climbing to 21/16 for The Gale over the same weeks — so the contrast
on screen is the one Kip is actually staring at, not a stand-in.

### 5.

Kip told Sofia that Meteor Mite has been texting all week asking if
something's broken, and the only answer Kip has is "hang in there."
Put that exchange on Meteor Mite's phone panel as an actual text
thread, and show what changes in it the moment the floor callout
fires: what Kip can tell her that isn't a guess anymore, and what she
sees on her own phone that tells her this was the system acting, not
luck.

### 6.

Open question 2 in the brief — the floor callout colliding with a
closer responder on a live incident — is still just a paragraph. Put
Kip in that exact moment on screen: an incident comes in, The Gale is
closer, Meteor Mite has hit the floor. Show the actual choice Kip has
to make between them, and what the screen looks like after Kip picks
either one.
